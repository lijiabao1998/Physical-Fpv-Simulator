# Stanford k1 experimental chronology inspection v1

Declared after the k1/25°C/1C parser pilot and before inspecting other raw workbooks or computing any Stanford model prediction. The original pilot selection is unchanged. This stage investigates recorded exposure history, not prediction accuracy.

Source: Catenaro and Onori, DOI 10.17632/kxsbr4x3j2.2, CC BY 4.0. Their data article, §1 and Table 3, defines k as the sample identifier across rates and temperatures. Select every official NMC k1 workbook: 15 files covering nominal rates 0.05, 1, 2, 3 and 5C at 5, 25 and 35°C. The manifest fixes their official byte counts, SHA256 digests and download links before inspection. Total compressed size is 35,182,688 bytes, including the already acquired pilot. No other cell is substituted based on results.

Bound the acquisition and parsing to 40,000,000 compressed bytes, 50,000,000 inflated ZIP bytes per workbook, and 600 seconds. Files are read sequentially with at most one workbook open. Verify each exact byte count and SHA256 before parsing; preserve original bytes. Reuse already verified files. Do not fetch the whole dataset archive or execute author scripts. Stop on budget, download-integrity or unsupported-schema failure and retain completed reports as partial evidence. A timeout or missing record is not proof of absent exposure.

Inspect every source row and contiguous protocol step. Preserve workbook measurement timestamps as naive local values. Repository upload dates and workbook creation metadata do not define experimental order. Report backwards measurement dates, disagreement between elapsed measurement dates and the source test clock, duplicate/conflicting times and overlapping workbook time intervals. For chronology qualification, require nondecreasing dates and a maximum relative clock discrepancy of 1 second, with no overlapping intervals for the same sample. This tolerance concerns timestamp consistency, not a physical model error gate. Preserve violations and mark ordering unresolved instead of repairing clocks.

Sort qualified workbook intervals by their first measurement timestamp. For the preselected k1/25°C/1C target, list every recorded experiment ending before its start and every overlapping record. Classify prior high-rate exposure from source labels above 1C and report actual current ranges and peak measured surface temperatures alongside those labels. Count earlier lower-rate and other-temperature records too. Do not infer that earlier cycling caused any later mismatch. Absence of an earlier published record cannot establish manufacturing, storage or unrecorded test history.

The publication identifies a separate laboratory and sample cohort from Chen/ORegan. That supports external provenance; it does not prove completely fresh cells, blinded predictions or lack of use by later parameter researchers. No material parameter, initial state, thermal coefficient, empirical threshold or curve is fitted in this stage. Source surface temperature remains a measured skin quantity; a lumped model average would require an explicit proxy label.

Stop after the 15-file chronology report, or at the stated limits. Preserve partial or conflicting evidence. No Stanford simulation is authorized by this protocol; freeze a separate prospective model comparison only after this inspection, with the original empirical gates and observed-time window intact.
